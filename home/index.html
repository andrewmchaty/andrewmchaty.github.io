Andrew McHaty.

Undergraduate researcher in physics and astrophysics.

I’m a Physics & Astrophysics double major at the University of California, Berkeley. My work connects astronomical observations, computational modeling, and experiments in the laboratory.

I study compact stellar systems and time-domain astronomy, develop tools for quantum-materials research, and have explored quantum-noise evolution in optical fibers at the University of Tokyo.

CURRENT WORK

Research at Berkeley

ASTROPHYSICS

Compact binaries & time-domain astronomy

Alex Filippenko’s research group · UC Berkeley

I’m developing a catalog of ultracompact eclipsing white dwarf binaries using ZTF, TESS, and Lick Observatory data to study their orbits, evolution, and potential as gravitational-wave sources. I also lead ZTF checking and help train undergraduate researchers to vet supernova and variable-star candidates.

QUANTUM MATERIALS

Experiments, automation & computation

James Analytis’ research group · UC Berkeley & Berkeley Lab

I develop an automated robotic storage system for quantum-material specimens and study crystal dissolution and formation through in-situ diffraction during flux growth. My work also explores machine-learning methods for separating crystalline peaks from background signals.

Simulating quantum noise in optical fibers
