ASTRODREWS / ABOUT ME

Hi, I'm Andrew.

Astrophotographer. Physics & astrophysics student. Researcher.

I'm Andrew McHaty, a Physics and Astrophysics double major at the University of California, Berkeley, with an expected graduation in May 2027. Through Astrodrews, I share the astronomical images I capture, the science behind them, and the techniques that bring distant objects into view.

My research spans astrophysics, quantum materials, and optical physics. I enjoy connecting observations with computation and hands-on experiments—from studying compact stellar systems to building tools for the laboratory.

What I'm working on

In Dr. Alex Filippenko's research group at Berkeley, I lead Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) checking, helping train undergraduate researchers to identify and vet supernova and variable-star candidates. I'm also developing a catalog of ultracompact eclipsing white dwarf binaries using ZTF, TESS, and Lick Observatory data to study their orbits, evolution, and potential as gravitational-wave sources.

With Dr. James Analytis' group at Berkeley and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, I develop an automated robotic storage system for quantum-material specimens. I also conduct in-situ diffraction experiments on crystal dissolution and formation during flux growth, and develop machine-learning methods to separate crystalline peaks from background signals.

Research across disciplines

During summer 2025, I worked with Dr. Takuro Ideguchi's Ultrafast Spectroscopy Group at the University of Tokyo, simulating Kerr squeezing and quantum-noise evolution in optical fibers and comparing simulations with experimental spectroscopy data.

My earlier research at Berkeley focused on exoplanet discovery: applying machine learning to Gaia astrometric data through the Mathematical & Physical Sciences Scholars REU, and using transit photometry with TESS and the Leuschner Telescope through the Undergraduate Lab at Berkeley (ULAB).

Sharing the night sky

I've been sharing astrophotography through Astrodrews since 2022. At Berkeley, I take part in sidewalk astronomy with the Undergraduate Astronomy Society, bringing telescopes into public spaces so more people can experience the night sky.

I previously served as the society's Astrophotography Officer and co-founded Astrophotography at Berkeley, leading workshops, image-processing tutorials, and observing trips. I also served as Research Director of ULAB's Physics & Astronomy division and continue to support students as a Physics Innovation Lab Superuser.

Education & recognition
